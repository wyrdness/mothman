# Mothman

## Overview

Mothman is the name given to a winged humanoid figure reported by more than 100 witnesses across the Point Pleasant area of West Virginia during a 13-month period beginning November 15, 1966. The first widely-reported sighting was made by Roger and Linda Scarberry and Steve and Mary Mallette near the abandoned West Virginia Ordnance Works (locally called the 'TNT area'). Witnesses described a roughly 7-foot, grey, humanoid creature with a wingspan of 10 feet and large, intensely glowing red eyes. The name 'Mothman' was coined by an Ohio copy editor as a riff on the popular Batman television series. Reports continued through 1967 and ended after the catastrophic collapse of the Silver Bridge on December 15, 1967, which killed 46 people and bound the creature to a harbinger-of-disaster framing in subsequent folklore. Journalist John Keel's investigations, published as The Mothman Prophecies (1975), tied Mothman to a wider 'Fortean' wave of UFO sightings, Men in Black encounters, and the entity 'Indrid Cold'. Skeptical explanations include misidentification of large North American owls (especially the barred owl) and sandhill cranes, with the eye reflectivity attributed to the tapetum lucidum of nocturnal birds. The town has since embraced Mothman as a cultural icon, with an annual festival and a museum in downtown Point Pleasant.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: The name 'Mothman' was coined by a copy editor at the Mason County Independent (or, in some accounts, an Athens, Ohio newspaper) drawing on the contemporary popularity of the Batman television series, which ran 1966–1968.
- **Meaning**: A portmanteau of 'moth' (for the wings) and 'man' (humanoid posture). Witnesses themselves had described 'a bird' or 'a man with wings'.
- **First use**: 1966-11 (Point Pleasant, West Virginia and surrounding press coverage)

## Notable Sightings

### Scarberry/Mallette TNT-area encounter ⭐
> **Date**: 1966-11-15  
> **Location**: Abandoned West Virginia Ordnance Works ('TNT area'), north of Point Pleasant  
> **Witnesses**: 4 (Civilian — two married couples)  
> **Evidence**: Witness statements to Mason County Sheriff; Point Pleasant Register coverage on 1966-11-16  
Roger and Linda Scarberry and Steve and Mary Mallette were driving through the TNT area around midnight when they saw a 6–7 ft tall grey humanoid with large folded wings and bright red glowing eyes near the abandoned generator plant. The creature reportedly took off vertically without flapping its wings and pursued their car at high speed back toward Point Pleasant. They reported the encounter to Deputy Millard Halstead.
**Credibility**: medium — The case that triggered the entire wave; effectively the canonical Mothman encounter.
**Sources**: Point Pleasant Register, 1966-11-16; Keel, J. (1975), The Mothman Prophecies, ch. 5

### Connie Carpenter encounter ⭐
> **Date**: 1966-11-25  
> **Location**: Near Mason, WV  
> **Witnesses**: 1 (Civilian (Connie Carpenter))  
> **Evidence**: Witness statement  
Connie Carpenter was driving on the morning of November 25, 1966 when she encountered the creature on the road. She reported being paralyzed by the gaze of the red eyes for the duration of the encounter, and described eye irritation (later described as sunburn-like) for days afterward.
**Credibility**: medium — Among the most cited individual encounters; central to Keel's account.
**Sources**: Keel, J. (1975), ch. 6

### Silver Bridge collapse and end of wave ⭐
> **Date**: 1967-12-15  
> **Location**: Silver Bridge, Point Pleasant, WV ↔ Kanauga, OH  
> **Witnesses**: 0 (N/A — disaster event, not a sighting)  
> **Evidence**: NTSB Highway Accident Report HAR-71-1 (1971); Press coverage 1967-12-15 onward  
The Silver Bridge, a 1928 eyebar suspension bridge, collapsed during evening rush hour, killing 46. NTSB later attributed the collapse to a single eyebar fatigue crack (NTSB HAR-71-1). Mothman sightings effectively ended with the collapse, and folkloric retellings frame the prior 13 months of sightings as harbingers of the disaster.
**Credibility**: high — Not a Mothman sighting but the historical anchor of the harbinger framing.
**Sources**: NTSB HAR-71-1 (1971); Keel, J. (1975), final chapters

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Fortean / interdimensional entity** _(John Keel, Loren Coleman)_

Mothman is a non-physical or interdimensional intelligence whose appearances form part of a wider 'window area' of UFO, MIB, and paranormal phenomena around Point Pleasant.

**Cornstalk's Curse** _(Local folklore)_

Local folklore frames Mothman as a manifestation of a curse placed on Point Pleasant by the Shawnee chief Cornstalk, murdered by U.S. militia in 1777.

### Skeptical

**Misidentified barred owl (Strix varia) or other large North American owl** _(Joe Nickell, Robert Smith (West Virginia University ornithology))_

Witnesses misidentified a startled barred owl (and possibly other Strigidae) flushing from the TNT-area woods. The species' large size, silent flight, glowing red eyeshine, and territorial behavior fit witness descriptions; the TNT area is documented owl habitat.

**Misidentified sandhill crane** _(Robert L. Smith (WVU))_

An out-of-range sandhill crane (Antigone canadensis) was seen in the area in late 1966 according to a contemporary West Virginia naturalist. Its large size (up to 1.2 m tall, 2 m wingspan) and red forehead patch could match witness reports.

**Hoax and mass-hysteria amplification** _(Joe Nickell, Benjamin Radford)_

The early reports were amplified by press coverage and the contemporary UFO wave, leading to expectation-driven sightings, copycat reports, and a feedback loop sustained by national media (Look magazine, etc.).
